\paragraph{Main comparison.}
We compare the seven planners under model mismatch, planning with $\theta_{\text{plan}}$ and executing with
$\theta_{\text{exec}}$, with $N = 2000$ simulations and a TV budget $\epsilon = 0.2$ for the robust planners
(Tables~\ref{tab:main-frozen-lake}, \ref{tab:main-cliffwalking} and \ref{tab:main-gambler}). On the two grid worlds,
the robust graph planners are the best methods for almost every pair $(\theta_{\text{plan}}, \theta_{\text{exec}})$:
GS-Robust-UCT and GSF-Robust-UCT come first, the nominal graph planners second, and the three tree planners last. The
gap widens as $\theta_{\text{exec}}$ grows, especially on CliffWalking, where the reward of the robust graph planners
decreases only slowly with the slip rate while that of every nominal planner falls sharply; neither merge dominates the
other. In contrast, Robust-UCT, which applies the same robust
backup on a tree, is no better than UCT and Power-UCT and is clearly
worse when $\theta_{\text{exec}}$ is small: without merging, each node receives too few
samples to estimate the next-state distribution on which the worst case is computed, so the robust backup only becomes
effective when samples are pooled in a graph. Gambler shows the limits of a fixed budget. When the planning model is
favourable ($\theta_{\text{plan}} \ge 0.6$), the nominal planners follow the timid strategy and lose almost every
episode when the coin turns out to be unfavourable, whereas GSF-Robust-UCT switches to the bold strategy and is the best
planner for $p_h < 0.5$, at the price of a lower reward when the coin is favourable. GS-Robust-UCT makes this switch
only partially, and at $\theta_{\text{plan}} = 0.2$ the budget $\epsilon = 0.2$ is so large that every robust value is
zero, so the robust planners there cannot rank the actions. Overall, combining the robust backup with state merging
gives the most reliable planner under model mismatch, and the benefit is largest when the execution environment is
much noisier than the planning model.
